Chess Learning Lessons

Study opening principles, tactical patterns, and endgame technique separately from your custom games. These original summaries are learning prompts, not a replacement for a full course.

LESSON 01 · BEGINNER

Opening principles

Build a sound position before launching an attack.

  1. Fight for the centre with pawns and pieces.
  2. Develop knights and bishops toward useful squares.
  3. Castle to help protect your king.
  4. Avoid moving the same piece repeatedly without a reason.
  5. Before every move, check whether a piece is hanging.
Practice task

Play a short opening and try to develop both knights and bishops while keeping your king safe.

LESSON 02 · TACTICS

Spot tactical opportunities

Look for forcing moves before choosing a quiet plan.

  • Fork: one piece attacks two or more targets.
  • Pin: a piece cannot safely move because a more valuable target is behind it.
  • Skewer: a valuable piece moves and exposes another target.
  • Discovered attack: moving one piece opens an attack from another.
  • Mate threat: calculate checks, escapes, and defensive replies.
Practice task

For each candidate move, ask: checks first, captures second, threats third. Then look for the opponent's strongest reply.

LESSON 03 · ENDGAME

Convert an endgame

When fewer pieces remain, king activity and pawn promotion become more important.

  • Activate your king rather than leaving it passive.
  • Support passed pawns and calculate the promotion race.
  • Use rook activity; rooks often belong behind passed pawns.
  • Learn opposition and key squares in king-and-pawn endings.
  • Do not assume a material advantage always wins; some positions are drawn.
Practice task

Review a king-and-pawn ending and identify whether the defending king can reach the promotion square.

LESSON 04 · STRATEGY

Make a positional plan

Improve your worst piece and identify the opponent's weaknesses.

  • Compare king safety and material.
  • Look for weak pawns, open files, and outposts.
  • Improve your least active piece.
  • Consider what your opponent wants to do next.
  • Do not launch an attack without enough pieces supporting it.
Practice task

Pause after development and write down one weakness to target and one opponent threat to prevent.

Free Study Library — available inside OpenChess

These quick-reference notes are written for this project and are visible here without leaving the app. The linked practice sites below also offer free learning resources; availability and account requirements may vary by feature.

Before every move
  1. What did my opponent's last move attack?
  2. Are my king or pieces in immediate danger?
  3. Can I give a useful check, capture, or direct threat?
  4. What is the opponent's strongest reply?
  5. After choosing, check that the moved piece is not left undefended.
Opening checklist
  1. Contest the centre.
  2. Develop minor pieces toward active squares.
  3. Castle when it is safe and useful.
  4. Connect the rooks.
  5. Avoid early queen adventures and unnecessary repeated moves.
Endgame checklist
  1. Activate the king.
  2. Calculate pawn races and promotion squares.
  3. Place rooks behind passed pawns when appropriate.
  4. Use opposition in king-and-pawn endings.
  5. Confirm whether the position is winning, drawn, or lost before trading pieces.
